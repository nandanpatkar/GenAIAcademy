Create specific, observable questions

Not all aspects of a customer interaction are equally important. A well-designed scoring system helps ensure your evaluations focus on what matters most to your business and customers.

In most customer interactions, certain elements have more impact than others. With weighting, you can assign different levels of importance to different questions or sections.

 

To set up scoring, do the following:

1.    Enable scoring for your evaluation form.

2.    Choose your Weight distribution mode, as follows:

 Weight by section: Distribute importance across major categories.

 Weight by question: Assign specific weights to individual questions.

3.    Assign percentage weights (total always adds up to 100%).

 

For example, you might set up a technical support evaluation form with the following section weights:

Customer verification (20%)

Technical troubleshooting (60%)

Contact wrap-up (20%)

Your technical support evaluation form could have a technical troubleshooting section, you might weight questions as follows:

Did the agent correctly identify the root cause? (25%)

Did the agent provide an accurate solution? (25%)

Did the agent verify that the solution worked? (10%)

This approach helps ensure that performance in the most critical aspects has a greater impact on the overall evaluation.

Not every question applies to every interaction. By using optional questions, you can maintain comprehensive evaluation forms while making sure agents are not penalized for questions that do not apply to their specific interaction.

 

To make a question optional, do the following:

Navigate to the question.

Select the Enable "Not Applicable" checkbox.

Save your changes.

When an evaluator marks a question as "Not Applicable," the question is removed from the scoring calculation and does not count toward the final score. The weights of the remaining questions are automatically recalculated proportionally.

 

For example, you have four questions weighted as follows:

Question 1: 40%

Question 2: 20%

Question 3: 20%

Question 4: 20%

If Question 4 is marked "Not Applicable," its 20% weight is redistributed as follows:

Question 1: 50% (40% × 100÷80)

Question 2: 25% (20% × 100÷80)

Question 3: 25% (20% × 100÷80)

Assigning scores to answers

Calculating Final Scores
Each answer option can be assigned a specific score, making it possible for you to create nuanced evaluations that reflect varying degrees of performance.

 

To assign scores to answers, do the following:

Navigate to the Scoring tab for your question.

Enter the Score value for each possible answer.

Ensure your scoring scale is consistent across similar questions.

For example:

For a Yes or No question: Yes = 10 points, No = 0 points

For more nuanced questions: Excellent = 10 points, Good = 7 points, Satisfactory = 5 points, Needs Improvement = 2 points, Poor = 0 points

Choose a scoring range that aligns with your overall approach, as follows:

0-10 scale: Offers enough granularity for most assessments

0-5 scale: Less complex but less nuanced

0-100 scale: Very granular but might be unnecessarily complex

Calculating Final Scores
The final evaluation score combines individual question scores based on their assigned weights. This weighted average provides a comprehensive assessment of agent performance.

 

The formula for calculating the final score is as follows:

Final score = Sum of (Question score X Question weight)

Understanding Evaluation Automation Options
Imagine you manage a busy contact center with hundreds of contacts coming in every day. How could you possibly listen to and evaluate every single interaction? You would need an army of people. This is where automated evaluations can help. Amazon Connect offers different types of automation to support your evaluation workflow. Each type is designed to address specific evaluation needs and provides different levels of human oversight.

The two types of automation offered in Amazon Connect are as follows.


Assisted evaluations
Automation does not have to mean replacing human judgment. In assisted evaluations, Amazon Connect provides automated answers to questions on evaluation forms while human evaluators are reviewing interactions. Think of it as having a smart assistant who suggests answers, but the evaluator still has the final say.

 

For example, when an evaluator is reviewing a call where the agent needs to verify the customer's identity, the automated system can scan the call transcript and suggest Yes to the question Did the agent properly verify the customer's identity? The evaluator can review this suggestion and either accept it or change it based on their own judgment.

 

This approach is particularly helpful for the following:

You want to speed up evaluations but maintain human oversight.

You are evaluating complex situations that might need human interpretation.

You are training new evaluators who need guidance on proper assessments.

The biggest advantage here is that evaluators do not have to start from scratch. The automation gives them a head start, making the process faster while still keeping humans in control. This leads to more consistent evaluations while still allowing for human judgment when needed.


Fully automated evaluations
With fully automated evaluations, Amazon Connect can automatically fill out all questions on an evaluation form and submit the completed evaluation without any human intervention.

 

For example, your evaluations could be configured to automatically evaluate 100% of customer interactions, checking if agents followed the required greeting script, maintained proper hold times, and resolved customer issues efficiently.

 

This approach works best for the following:

You need to evaluate a large volume of contacts.

You want consistent application of objective standards.

You are tracking straightforward compliance requirements.

Even with fully automated evaluations, human evaluators can still review, edit, and resubmit evaluations if needed. This provides a safety net for catching any potential issues with the automated assessments.

Automation Methods
Amazon Connect offers three different methods to power automated evaluations. Each method has specific strengths that make it suitable for different types of evaluation questions.

The three options for automation offered in Amazon Connect are as follows.


Metrics-Based Automation

Rules-Based Automation

Generative AI Automation
Metrics are measurable aspects of a contact center interaction. Think of metrics as the numbers and statistics that tell you what happened during a customer interaction. Amazon Connect can automatically answer questions based on these numerical values.

 

For example, if your evaluation form asks how long the customer was on hold, Amazon Connect can automatically fill in the exact hold time from the contact record data.

 

Common metrics used in automated evaluations include the following:

Contact duration

Hold time (total and longest single instance)

Transfer rate

Customer sentiment score

Response time

Silence time

Metrics-based automation works best for questions that have clear numerical answers. There is no interpretation needed, just facts and figures.

 

For instance, if your company policy states that holds should never exceed 2 minutes, you could have an evaluation question that asks, "Did the agent keep hold times under 2 minutes?" The system would automatically answer based on the actual hold time data.

 

The main limitation of metrics is that they only work for questions with numerical answers. They cannot tell you if an agent was empathetic or if they resolved the customer's issue correctly.

Rules-Based Automation

Generative AI Automation
Rules-based automation uses Amazon Connect Contact Lens categories (predefined rules) to automatically answer single-selection questions on your evaluation form.

 

For example, you could create a rule to detect if agents use the proper greeting, "Thank you for calling AnyCompany. My name is [agent name]. How may I help you today?" Then, evaluation questions about proper greetings would be automatically answered based on whether that greeting was detected.

 

Common applications for rules-based automation include the following:

Compliance phrases ("This call may be recorded")

Required greetings and closings

Prohibited language detection

Identity verification steps

Mandatory disclaimers

Rules work by scanning the transcript for specific phrases, patterns, or keywords. They are perfect for black-and-white questions where something either happened or did not happen.

 

The key consideration with rules is that they only work well for detecting specific phrases or patterns. They cannot understand context or make judgment calls about the quality of an interaction.

Now that you have reviewed rules-based automation, move on to the next tab to learn about generative AI automation.

Generative AI Automation
Generative AI represents the most advanced form of evaluation automation. It can understand and analyze conversations more like a human would, going beyond basic metrics or keyword matching.

 

With generative AI, Amazon Connect can answer both single-selection questions (Yes or No, multiple choice) and text field questions that require written responses.

 

For example, if your evaluation form asks, "Did the agent show empathy toward the customer's situation?" generative AI can analyze the full conversation. It will evaluate context, tone, and specific responses to determine if empathy was demonstrated.

 

Generative AI is particularly useful for evaluating the following:

Problem resolution effectiveness

Agent soft skills (empathy, professionalism)

Customer satisfaction indicators

Complex compliance scenarios

Quality of explanations and information provided

When using generative AI, you provide evaluation criteria in natural language, and the AI interprets those instructions to evaluate the conversation. Along with answers, generative AI provides context, justification, and references to specific parts of the transcript.

 

Contact Lens generative AI can currently evaluate information contained within Amazon Connect transcripts.

Setting Up Calibration Sessions
Calibration is a structured process where multiple managers independently evaluate the same customer contact using the same evaluation form, with the goal of ensuring consistency, fairness, and accuracy in how agent performance is assessed.

Performing calibration evaluations

When you participate in a calibration session, your goal is to evaluate the interaction objectively and independently. You should also follow the established criteria exactly as you would in your regular evaluation work. Optionally, evaluation responses could be compared against the expert (Quality manager) responses during calibration sessions to benchmark evaluation accuracy.

The following checklist outlines important steps to consider when performing calibration evaluations. Select each option as you complete them.

Review the contact thoroughly

Take notes on specific moments that influence your scoring decisions.

Review any relevant reference materials or evaluation criteria before scoring.

Focus exclusively on this specific interaction, not the agent's past performance.


Completing the evaluation form

Answer all questions based strictly on the established criteria.

Provide detailed notes explaining your reasoning for each score.

Support your scores with concrete examples from the interaction.

Remember that automation is disabled on calibration sessions.

Be particularly attentive to scoring definitions for each question.


Maintaining objectivity

Focus exclusively on what happened in this specific interaction.

Reference specific evaluation criteria when making decisions.

Support your scores with concrete examples from the interaction.

Consider the entire interaction before finalizing scores.

Summary
You have now equipped yourself with powerful tools to transform raw evaluation data into meaningful coaching opportunities and performance improvements.

Remember that the true value of evaluations is not in the scores themselves, but in how you use them to drive positive change. The most effective supervisors do not just collect data, they tell stories with it, celebrate successes, and guide improvements with empathy and purpose.

As you implement these practices, keep the following key principles in mind:

Be timely: Share feedback as soon as possible after evaluations are completed.

Be specific: Focus on concrete behaviors rather than general impressions.

Be balanced: Recognize strengths alongside areas for improvement.

Be actionable: Make sure agents know exactly which steps to take to improve.

By learning these evaluation analysis and feedback techniques, you're building a contact center culture that values continuous improvement and meaningful coaching. Your agents will appreciate the clarity and fairness this brings to performance management, and your customers will benefit from the consistently improving service experience.
