Lesson introduction

IVR solutions have optimized the way businesses interact with their customers over the phone. These automated telephony systems use voice and touch-tone inputs to streamline operations, enhance efficiency, and improve customer service. This lesson explores details of IVR technology, its benefits, core concepts, considerations, and real-world applications.

Interactive voice response introduction

An IVR is a technology that automates interactions with callers using voice and touch-tone keypad inputs. It serves as an automated telephony system that interacts with callers, gathers information, and routes calls based on predefined criteria. 

In its traditional usage, an IVR greets callers with a recorded message that provides instructions on how to proceed. Callers navigate through the system by responding to prompts using keypad inputs. For example, a common scenario is when a contact calls a bank's customer service line and is greeted by an IVR. The IVR prompts them to choose options like account inquiries, payments, or speaking with a representative.  

IVR is designed to handle various tasks, such as providing information about products, processing payments, and directing callers to the appropriate agent. 

By automating these routine tasks, IVR help organizations do the following:

•
Streamline operations.

•
Reduce call wait times.

•
Improve customer service efficiency.

In addition to standard menu-driven IVR, technology has introduced features like natural language processing (NLP) and speech recognition. These features provide conversational interactions with callers. Modern IVR can understand and respond to natural language queries, enhancing the overall user experience and making interactions more intuitive and seamless.

Lesson introduction

IVRs offer self-service and routing capabilities that streamline interactions and optimize resource utilization within organizations. These features empower customers to resolve their queries autonomously while ensuring that complex issues get routed to the appropriate agents or departments efficiently. 

By offering self-service options and intelligent call routing, IVRs empower customers, improve operational efficiency, and enhance the overall customer experience. This increases customer satisfaction and loyalty.

Self-service

An IVR provides customers with various self-service options, so they can access information or complete transactions without the assistance of an agent.

Call routing

Effective call routing is an important aspect of an IVR. It ensures that incoming calls get directed to the most suitable agent or department, based on the customer's needs. 

The IVR prompts callers to provide specific information, such as account details, the reason for their call, or the desired service. With this input, the system routes the call to the appropriate queue or agent with the relevant skills to handle the inquiry efficiently. 

This routing process minimizes unnecessary transfers and reduces the time customers spend navigating through various departments or agents.


Advanced IVR integrations with CRM systems and databases can further enhance routing capabilities. By retrieving customer information, the system personalizes the contact experience. It routes calls based on the contact's demographic profile, preferences, and past interactions. 



This level of integration ensures that customers are routed to the agents who have the necessary context and background information. Overall, it leads to more efficient and personalized service.

IVRs can also incorporate skill-based routing. This means the IVR can collect data to help find the agents with the most relevant expertise or language proficiency for each contact. This functionality is particularly valuable for organizations with diverse product lines, services, or multilingual customer bases. It ensures that customers receive specialized assistance tailored to their specific needs.

Lesson introduction

Amazon Lex and Amazon Connect are two cloud-based services offered by Amazon Web Services (AWS) that work together to provide organizations with advanced IVR and contact center capabilities. In this lesson, you will learn about how Amazon Connect and Amazon Lex interact.

Amazon Lex


Amazon Lex is a service for building conversational interfaces using voice and text. It uses automatic speech recognition (ASR) and NLP technologies to allow applications to understand and respond to natural language inputs. IVR designers can create conversational bots, or chatbots, using Amazon Lex and define the logic and responses for various intents and utterances. These bots can be integrated into applications, websites, and voice-enabled devices, enabling natural language interactions with users.

Amazon Connect

Amazon Connect is a service that empowers organizations to set up and manage customer communication channels, including voice, chat, and task management. It provides tools for handling customer interactions, such as ACD, IVR, real-time monitoring and reporting.


Integration of Amazon Lex and Amazon Connect

Amazon Lex and Amazon Connect exchange information to deliver natural language-based IVR experiences for interactions. The built-in integration between Amazon Connect and Amazon Lex empowers organizations to incorporate conversational AI bots into their contact center workflows.

When a customer calls a contact center equipped with Amazon Connect, the IVR powered by Amazon Lex can engage in a natural language conversation. The Amazon Lex bot can understand the contact's intent and gather relevant information, such as account details or the reason for the call.

Based on the input and defined logic, the Amazon Lex bot can do the following:

Handle the request directly by providing information.
Perform actions.
Route the call to the most appropriate agent or department.
This routing is facilitated by integrating Amazon Lex with Amazon Connect call routing capabilities.

The integration provides a transition between the conversational bot and an agent. When an Amazon Lex bot determines that human assistance is required, it transfers the call to an available agent, along with the conversation context. This context transfer ensures that agents have access to relevant details, reducing the need for customers to repeat information and enhancing the overall customer experience.

Integration of Amazon Lex and Lambda

Amazon Lex can be enhanced to create sophisticated bots that understand and respond to natural language inputs.

Amazon Lex integrates with Lambda, which extends the ability to add custom logic to your bot's responses. When a user interacts with your bot, Amazon Lex recognizes the user's intent and sends the user input to a Lambda function. The Lambda function performs the required actions. Examples include querying databases, calling external web services, or performing complex calculations. The Lambda function then returns a response to Amazon Lex. Amazon Lex formats the response in a conversational way and sends it back to the user.

Using Lambda with Amazon Lex provides several advantages:

First, it separates Amazon Lex's NLP capabilities from the application logic, making it more convenient to maintain and update your bot.
Second, you can use other AWS services and third-party applications in your bot's logic. For example, you can use Lambda to interact with other AWS services like Amazon DynamoDB, Amazon Simple Storage Service (Amazon S3), or other external services. This expands the range of functionality, and your bot can perform a much wider range of tasks.
Screenshot of Amazon Lex configuration for an AWS Lambda function.
Lex configuration interface for a help desk agent bot

When you create a Lex bot, you can specify the Lambda function that is invoked for each intent. The bot input and any relevant context are passed to the Lambda function as input parameters. The Lambda function can then process the input, perform any necessary actions, and return a response to Lex, which formats and delivers the response to the user.

By combining the NLP capabilities of Amazon Lex with the flexibility of Lambda, you can create highly sophisticated conversational bots. These bots can use custom logic to achieve your business objectives.

Lesson introduction

When designing an IVR, several important considerations must be taken into account to ensure an effective and user-friendly experience. In this lesson, you will review these considerations.

Overview

One of the factors to consider when designing an IVR is the clarity and ease of use of the menu options. Users should be able to navigate the IVR naturally to reach their desired destination. This implies organizing options logically and using concise, clear language to guide callers through the menu.

It is essential to find a balance between automation and human intervention. Although automation can streamline processes and reduce wait times, there are instances where human assistance might be necessary. Adding the option to speak with an agent at any point during the interaction ensures that the contact promptly receives the assistance they need.

Personalization and customization play an important role in IVR design. Tailoring the IVR experience to the specific needs and preferences of the audience enhances satisfaction. This includes personalizing greetings, offering language options, and providing self-service capabilities for common tasks, such as bill payment or account inquiries. By offering a customized experience, callers are likely to feel valued and understood, leading to improved engagement and loyalty.

Another important consideration in IVR design is accessibility. Implementing features like speech recognition, keypad input alternatives, and TTS ensures that callers can interact with the system effectively and autonomously.

In summary, effective IVR design requires careful attention to various factors, including menu clarity, automation compared to human intervention, customization, and accessibility. By prioritizing these considerations, organizations can create an IVR that enhances satisfaction and improves efficiency.

Choosing between bots and DTMF in IVRs

(opens in a new tab)(opens in a new tab)

When deciding between using bots and DTMF in IVR, the following are some key considerations to determine the most suitable approach for specific use cases.


Complexity of interaction
Bots excel in handling complex interactions that require understanding user intent and following conversation flows. They are ideal for scenarios where users need personalized assistance or when open-ended questions are common. In contrast, DTMF is suitable for interactions where users only need to input numbers or make straightforward selections from menus.


User preference
Consider the preferences of your contacts. If they prefer a conversational and natural language experience, using bots can enhance user satisfaction. If users prioritize a quick and direct interaction method, DTMF provides a fast and efficient way for them to navigate through options.


Flexibility and customization
Bots offer greater flexibility and customization options compared to DTMF. They can handle a wider range of data types beyond just numbers, collect detailed information, and provide personalized responses based on user inputs. DTMF, on the other hand, is limited to numeric inputs and predefined menu selections.


Integration and automation
Evaluate the integration capabilities and automation requirements of your IVR. Bots can integrate with other systems and APIs, providing automated workflows and data retrieval. DTMF interactions are typically more straightforward and might require less integration.


Contextual understanding
Bots have the advantage of contextual understanding, where they can maintain conversation context across multiple interactions. This provides a more personalized and continuous user experience compared to DTMF interactions, which are often transactional.

Amazon Lex session attributes

Amazon Connect is a client application that initiates sessions with Amazon Lex using the Get customer input flow block. Developers have an option to include a chatbot in a conversation by using the Amazon Lex APIs. Amazon Lex session attributes are key value pairs that store and carry information throughout the duration of a session. Session attributes contain application-specific information that is passed between a bot and a client application during a session. Amazon Lex maintains context and personalizes responses based on user input and the logic implemented for various types of conversations.

Similar to the Amazon Connect contact attributes, Amazon Lex session attributes store user-specific data, or contextual details relevant to the ongoing conversation. Amazon Lex session attributes are dynamic and can be modified based on user inputs, system actions, or external data sources. This flexibility allows Amazon Lex to dynamically adapt responses and actions for contacts.

Setting Amazon Lex attributes from Amazon Connect

Session attributes in Amazon Lex play an important role in managing conversation state and flow. They help track the progression of the conversation, manage branching logic, and select appropriate actions. For example, you can configure the maximum duration of utterances, the maximum length of a numeric input, or an end character for DTMF input. 

To assign values to Amazon Lex session attributes, use the Get customer input block configuration panel in the Amazon Connect flow. Select the Amazon Lex tab, then choose Add an attribute for each attribute you want to set.

Interrupt behavior
This defines whether the user can interrupt the prompt that solicits input. You can selectively turn it off by setting the value of the attribute to False. 



The interrupt functionality is also referred to as barge-in, and it is enabled by default. You can disable it in the Amazon Lex console. You can also modify the barge-in behavior by using the following allow-interrupt session attribute. 



x-amz-lex:allow-interrupt:<intentName>:<slotName>


Default value: True.



To configure interrupts for all intents and slots, replace the <intentName>  and <slotName> with an asterisk:



x-amz-lex:allow-interrupt:*:*

Maximum utterance length
This defines how long the bot waits before speech input is truncated and the speech is returned to your application. You can increase the length of the input when you expect long responses, or if you want to give customers more time to provide information. To do so, use the following attribute, and set the value for your configuration setting.



x-amz-lex:audio:max-length-ms:<intentName>:<slotName>
Default value: 13,000 milliseconds (13 seconds). The maximum value is 15,000 milliseconds (15 seconds) 


Voice timeout
Defines how long a bot waits before assuming that the customer isn't going to speak. You can increase the time in situations where the customer might need more time to find or recall information before speaking. For example, you might want to give customers time to get out their credit card so they can enter the number.



x-amz-lex:audio:start-timeout-ms:<intentName>:<slotName>


Default value: 4,000 milliseconds (4 seconds)

Overview
IVR is an automated telephony solution that uses voice and touch-tone inputs to streamline operations, enhance efficiency, and improve experiences. Traditional IVR relied on DTMF input, where callers navigated through menus by pressing numbers on their phone's keypad. In contrast, modern IVR systems use advanced speech recognition and NLP technologies, giving callers the ability to interact using conversational language.

IVR design considerations
Effective IVR design requires careful attention to various factors, including menu clarity, automation compared to human intervention, customization, and accessibility. Clarity and ease of use of menu options, personalization and customization, and ensuring accessibility for individuals with disabilities are crucial considerations. Deciding between using conversational bots or DTMF input depends on different factors. Consider interaction complexity, user preferences, flexibility requirements, scalability considerations, integration needs, and the desired level of contextual understanding when designing experiences.

Capturing contact input
Within the Amazon Connect contact flow editor, administrators design the IVR menu by adding blocks like Play prompt, Get customer input, or Transfer to queue. The Get customer input block prompts callers to provide specific information or make selections using their keypad or voice. Administrators can configure various input types, validation rules, and conditional logic to personalize the call experience based on the customer's input.

Amazon Connect and Amazon Lex data exchange
When a contact engages with an Amazon Connect flow that is integrated with Amazon Lex, their input is captured and transmitted to Amazon Lex. It employs predefined intents and slots to process the customer's input, identifying the purpose of the interaction and any specific details provided. Amazon Lex formulates a response, which is forwarded to Amazon Connect, enabling dynamic adjustment of the IVR experience. It also assists with call routing, personalized assistance, or automated task performance based on the conversation context. Throughout the interaction, Amazon Lex and Amazon Connect exchange data in real time, ensuring updated information and context retention.
